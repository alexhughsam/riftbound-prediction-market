The marketTwo forces, one conclusion

Every price move in this game since launch traces back to a tug-of-war between real, growing demand and a publisher that refuses to let scarcity form in anything it can reprint.

Demand is real and growing

  • T1 Worlds Collection confirmed — revealed at MSI Seoul: gold-stamped player autographs including Faker, one serialized /2025 card per set, fixed at 10,125 sets per language. August drawing, $360.
  • Radiance (Oct 23) is the de facto K/DA set — music-themed, Seraphine/Evelynn/Ekko, with Akali landing in Vendetta. K/DA's comeback is confirmed for Season 3.
  • Korean market opens September — a whole new demand pool for Origins chase cards.
  • First Worlds confirmed for 2027, with regionals in Barcelona, LA, and Singapore this fall feeding into it.
  • Chase signatures appreciated 20–70% through 2026 while populations of graded copies remain tiny.

Riot suppresses reprintable scarcity

  • Stated policy: reprint early sets "until the market reaches healthy quantities." Origins boxes fell 45% in nine weeks when the wave hit.
  • No first-print premium can form — reprints are deliberately indistinguishable; Riot blind-tested them.
  • The Flash precedent: when a banned-list shakeup spiked a $72 staple, Riot mailed free copies to every game store. Price halved within weeks.
  • Direct sales with lottery drawings and per-account caps cut flippers out of every new release.
  • The Lorcana analog looms: first-set chase cards there round-tripped 40–70% once supply and graded populations caught up.

Riftbound has One Piece–level demand and Lorcana-level supply policy. Buy what Riot cannot reprint; pay MSRP or nothing for what it can.The working thesis

The planHow to actually start

This is a high-risk, illiquid asset class with a ~13% exit toll. The sizing rules matter more than the picks: use only money you can lose entirely, cap the whole position around 5% of what you invest overall, and enter in stages — roughly a third now, a third into the August–October catalyst windows, and a third held for the dips that reprint announcements reliably create. The Origins arc showed what buying the top costs: anyone who bought boxes in March was down 45% by May.

A model first allocation

  • Non-reprintable scarcity40%
    T1 Signature Edition via the August drawing, event promos, Worlds bundles. The only structurally supply-capped assets in the game — and where the community's verified wins keep coming from.
  • Sealed at MSRP only25%
    Vendetta and Radiance displays at $119.99 through Riot drawings, LGS preorders, retail drops. Never above MSRP — the downside stays bounded and you never fight the printer.
  • Graded chase singles20%
    PSA 10 signatures while populations sit in the single and double digits. Grading only pencils out above ~$150 raw value against an $80 grading fee.
  • Meta singles — trading, not holding10%
    Buy staples ahead of the fall regionals, sell into result spikes. Take profits fast — remember what Riot did to Flash.
  • Cash reserve5%
    Reprint-announcement dips are a recurring, purchasable event in this game.

The four mistakes that cost the most

  1. Buying street-priced sealed of old sets. Origins at $183 is still under active reprint pressure, and Riot has said more is coming.
  2. Holding playable staples long. Flash went $72 → ~$40 after Riot mailed free copies to every store. Any spiking staple is a promo-print away from a haircut.
  3. Paying secondary prices for the T1 set. Enter the drawing at $360. Losing the lottery is not a reason to pay a flipper $700 in September.
  4. Ignoring the exit math. A card must rise ~15% just to break even after fees. This quietly kills small-position trading of cheap cards.

Measure it, don't vibe it

The repo is the measurement system: log every purchase in the portfolio file the day you buy, let the daily price snapshot accrue history automatically, and check the portfolio valuation weekly, not daily — illiquid assets punish twitchy watching. Set two review checkpoints — after Radiance in November, after Convergence Fest in January — and compare profit per bucket, after fees. Scale up only what's working; write the exit thesis for every position down when you open it, so future-you remembers why it exists.

ToolkitWhat's built and what's still missing

The repository now covers the daily loop: a price snapshotter pulling every Riftbound single and sealed product from the free tcgcsv mirror of TCGplayer (category 89, verified), a movers report with watchlist buy/sell alerts, portfolio profit tracking, per-product price history, and a GitHub Action that snapshots daily once merged to main.

The ranked gaps that remain: historical backfill (tcgcsv publishes archived daily dumps back to launch), a PriceCharting subscription (~$6/month — the only realistic source of eBay sold prices and graded population data for an individual), an announcement-feed alerter on Riot's news page (bans and reprints move prices within days), and restock alerts for MSRP buying.
